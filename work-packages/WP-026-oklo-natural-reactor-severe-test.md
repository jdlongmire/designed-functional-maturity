## 1. Purpose

Use the Oklo natural fission reactors as a severe test of DFM radiogenic initialization and compressed historical reconstruction.

Oklo is unusually valuable because the evidence network joins:

1. directly measured uranium-isotope depletion;
2. fission-product isotope patterns;
3. neutron-fluence and reactor-physics reconstruction;
4. selective radionuclide retention and migration;
5. U-Pb and other isotope-system chronology;
6. the differential-decay relation between U-235 and U-238;
7. mineralogical and hydrothermal history.

The programme shall neither dismiss Oklo as "just dating" nor treat the conventional age as a raw observation.

## 2. Core epistemic decomposition

```text
present isotope/mineral state
+ operational nuclear physics
+ reactor model
+ initial-state / closure / transport auxiliaries
-> reconstructed reactor history
-> historical elapsed-time interpretation
```

The existence of past natural fission is a distinct inference from the elapsed time assigned to that fission event.

## 3. Severe-test questions

### Q1 — Fission reality
Can the observed U depletion, fissiogenic isotope distributions, neutron-capture signatures, and Pu contribution be explained without sustained natural criticality?

Default appraisal: natural criticality is strongly supported and is not a DFM target for denial.

### Q2 — Criticality conditions
Given ordinary reactor physics, what U-235 abundance, geometry, moderation, neutron poisons, temperature, and burnup are required by the measured state?

### Q3 — Differential uranium decay
Does the present U-235/U-238 ratio, evolved backward under measured decay constants, independently recover the fissile abundance required by reactor physics?

This is a high-value convergence node and a direct burden on compressed chronology.

### Q4 — Transport and closure
Which isotope systems were retained, which migrated, over what spatial scales, and with what mineralogical controls?

"Open system" may be invoked only element-by-element and mechanism-by-mechanism. Selective retention forbids a generic disturbance escape hatch.

### Q5 — Chronology
Which portions of the conventional ~2 Ga reconstruction arise from:
- directly measured present state;
- measured decay constants;
- reactor-physics constraints;
- isotope-system retrodiction;
- geological ordering;
- closure/transport models;
- cross-system convergence?

### Q6 — DFM generative burden
Can an independently specified DFM initial state and subsequent history reproduce the Oklo network without importing a conventional age or an age-equivalent scalar?

## 4. Anti-circularity rule

DFM receives no predictive credit if it initializes the Oklo uranium ratio, daughter products, or fission inventory from values calculated using the conventional reactor age and then recovers the same structure.

Required workflow:

```text
SPECIFY -> RUN -> PREDICT -> COMPARE
```

Forbidden workflow:

```text
OBSERVE -> RETROCALCULATE AGE -> INITIALIZE FROM AGE -> RECOVER OBSERVATION
```

## 7. Initial appraisal

### Conventional comparator

The conventional account has a genuine explanatory success if one elapsed-time reconstruction simultaneously accounts for:

- a higher earlier natural U-235 fraction under differential U decay;
- the fissile abundance independently required for criticality;
- fission and neutron-capture products;
- geological and isotope-system ordering.

The dependency graph must determine how independent those constraints actually are.

### DFM

Current DFM does not yet possess a quantitative Oklo reconstruction. Therefore Oklo begins as a **severe challenge / active debit** to the current compressed-history auxiliary until a specified model is run.

Possible isotope mobility is relevant but insufficient. DFM must explain both mobility and retention.

## 8. Failure criteria

Current DFM radiogenic/chronological auxiliaries receive substantial negative pressure if:

1. no age-independent initialization can produce the required U-235 inventory;
2. a compressed post-initialization history cannot physically generate the observed fission/neutron-capture network;
3. required isotope disturbance contradicts observed selective retention;
4. explaining Oklo requires unrelated datum-by-datum initialization;
5. a latent conventional-age parameter is required;
6. proposed nuclear-constant changes conflict with Oklo neutron-capture constraints or independent operational physics.

A failure of one DFM auxiliary does not falsify creation or design as such. It falsifies or weakens the tested auxiliary.

## 9. Progression criteria

DFM gains standing if it independently predicts:

- the required criticality-compatible U inventory;
- coupled fission-product and neutron-capture signatures;
- element-specific retention/migration patterns;
- a bounded Oklo-specific discriminator not used in model construction;
- cross-isotope relationships without age-equivalent tuning.

## 11. Canonical disposition

> Oklo is accepted as evidence of past natural nuclear fission. Its conventional chronology is a model-conditioned historical reconstruction with unusually strong cross-constraints. DFM must account for the entire coupled nuclear-geochemical network, including the differential uranium-decay/criticality convergence and selective radionuclide transport. Until it does so quantitatively, Oklo remains a severe challenge rather than positive evidence for DFM.
