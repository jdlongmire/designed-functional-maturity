# WP-002: Foundations paper — actualization, agency, and intelligibility

**Target artifact:** `drafts/foundations-actualization-agency-intelligibility.md`
**Status:** Draft complete — pending review

## Framing

WP-001's paper is *comparative-methodological*: it surveys ten foundational features of reality and argues methodological designism absorbs them with fewer residual relocations than methodological naturalism. That paper assumes without argument the thing this paper defends: that actualization is a real explanatory demand, and that agency is the category that answers it.

This is the third paper in the programme, and logically the first:

1. **Foundations** (this WP) — why actualization needs explaining and why agency is the proposed primitive
2. **Comparative method** (WP-001) — naturalism vs. designism across ten features
3. **Historical claim** (planned) — whether Genesis 1 describes an actual deployment sequence

## Objective

State the foundational argument at a rigor sufficient to survive its strongest published opposition, and be explicit where it does not survive.

## Scope

1. **Split actualization and intelligibility into two sequential arguments.** The source essay runs them together. Actualization gets you to *an* agent; intelligibility is what gets you to a *rational* agent. Running them separately is both more honest and more effective, and it protects the intelligibility argument from objections that only bite the actualization argument.
2. **Ground "constraint is not actualization" in non-theist sources.** Hawking raises the gap and leaves it open; Albert presses it against Krauss from within naturalism; Parfit and Smolin make adjacent moves. The central claim must not rest only on theist advocates.
3. **Engage the real opposition, named.** Grünbaum (the question is ill-posed), van Inwagen (PSR entails modal collapse; and the probabilistic dissolution), Oppy (taxonomy-plus-parity with an Ockham premium). Not the naive "who caused God."
4. **Make the luck/selection objection the paper's central burden.** Agent causation is a standing category (Reid, Chisholm, O'Connor, Lowe), but the contrastive-explanation regress reattaches at the agent. The paper must argue that non-contrastive agent-causal explanation is still explanation, and say what that costs.
5. **Run the intelligibility argument as abduction, not self-defeat.** Fitelson & Sober's objections bite Plantinga's self-defeat version hard and barely touch a best-explanation version (Reppert's distinction; Lewis's own 1960 revision after Anscombe).
6. **Hedge the quantum-cosmology section hard.** Existential form only ("not *required* by"), canonical quantization specifically, minisuperspace caveat, DeWitt's own repudiation, and pre-empt the "you have rediscovered general covariance" reply.
7. **State a fabrication criterion and scope it correctly.** Distinguish required-for-commissioned-function from gratuitous event-record, partition claims into initialization state / real history under contested reconstruction / prochronic event-record, and apply the criterion only where it belongs. Report the residual honestly wherever it lands.
8. **Name discriminators.** The programme's other research lines run Popper/Lakatos ladders. A foundations paper with no falsification conditions fails the programme's own standard.

## Notes (2026-08-21)

Six parallel research passes covering: constraint-vs-actualization in non-theist sources; Wheeler-DeWitt and the problem of time; PSR/contingency and its strongest counters; Omphalos and the apparent-age literature; the intelligibility/reliability argument and its critics; agent causation as an irreducible category.

Two findings changed the paper's shape rather than merely supplying citations.

**The luck/selection objection is not answerable by the move the framework most naturally reaches for.** Agent causation is a genuine standing category with a real lineage, and O'Connor has already applied it to the cosmological question specifically, so the framework is not inventing anything. But the contrastive regress reattaches: "why did the agent actualize *this*?" The literature's dominant response is to deny that contrastive explanation is owed, not to supply one. §4.3 makes that argument explicitly and states its cost rather than burying it.

**The fabrication criterion, applied honestly, gives a split verdict on light in transit.** The distinction between required-for-function and gratuitous event-record has been asserted in popular creationist writing since at least Harold (2017) but never developed philosophically; Duff (2021) argues it collapses precisely because no one has made it functional. Illumination is required-for-function; structured event-records in the light (supernova curves, eclipsing-binary curves) are not. Faulkner (2026), writing as Answers in Genesis' own astronomer, concedes the same. §8.5 reports this and leaves it open.

## Revision, 2026-08-21 (JD correction)

**§8 was restructured after JD identified a framing error: DFM plus an auxiliary hypothesis is not Omphalos, and the original §8 conflated two structurally different things.**

The error: the draft treated functional maturity as the model's answer to *all* apparent-age data, including the fossil record. It is not. Gosse's *prochronic* markers are records of events that never occurred. A catastrophist reconstruction says the opposite about fossils, namely that they are *diachronic* records of real organisms that really died, deposited by a contested mechanism on a contested timescale. That is a competing historical reconstruction making falsifiable claims, and the deceiver objection has no purchase on it because nothing false is recorded.

§8 now partitions claims into three classes: **A**, initialization state (records nothing; boundary conditions are not claims about the past); **B**, real history under a contested reconstruction (fossils, strata, and — a case the original draft misfiled — radiometric ratios, since an initialized R₀ is a boundary condition rather than a record); **C**, genuinely prochronic event-records. The criterion adjudicates the A/C boundary only and is not the relevant test for B.

New §8.4 uses the Catastrophic Hydrotectonic Flood Model as the worked example, on three structural grounds verified against the repository directly rather than characterized from memory: hard-core commitment 5 explicitly commits to lawful production of the record and states that this "is what makes the belt testable at all"; registered exclusions E1 and E2 forbid real mechanisms and bind the programme itself ("this programme is not exempt"), with E2 ruling against a rival within the same family; and the rheology gate holds an unresolved three-to-four-order-of-magnitude shortfall in the open, with the programme stating that conceding it "would itself be a real result." Omphalos is structurally incapable of any of the three. The paper takes no position on whether the reconstruction is correct, only on what kind of claim it is.

§8.5 preserves the light-in-transit residual but narrows it correctly and notes that the flood programme does not reach it by design, since its own four-tier scope statement assigns astronomical initialization upstream to DFM. §8.6 adds a discriminator that guards the new defense: if Class B auxiliary hypotheses cease to forbid anything, §8.4's argument lapses and the model should be judged as Omphalos in different vocabulary. The distinction is earned by a programme's behavior, not conferred by its subject matter.
