# WP-002 — Retrodictive Age Terminology and Radiometric Inference Cleanup

Status: approved for execution
Parent decision: BWM WP-BWM-0022

## Decision

DFM adopts **retrodictive age** (`t_R`) as the technical term for a past time or elapsed duration inferred by applying a chronometric model to present measurements. **Extrapolated age** is the preferred plain-language gloss.

DFM distinguishes `t_R` from **actual elapsed age** (`t_H`) and from the **initialization state** (`S_0`).

`t_R != necessarily t_H`

The measurement is real. The historical duration is an inference from that measurement under a model.

## Radiometric formulation

Let `M` denote the measured isotopic state and `C` the chronometric inference model:

`M --C--> t_R`

For an initialized system, DFM asks whether:

`M = f(S_0, delta t_H, system history)`

can yield a retrodictive age different from actual elapsed history.

This question does not license arbitrary initial conditions. Initial radionuclide inventories, daughter-product distributions, isochrons, short-lived radionuclide systems, thermal budgets, reset histories, and cross-method concordance require independent physical analysis.

## Cleanup

- Use retrodictive age for model-derived chronological age in active DFM technical work.
- Use extrapolated age as the public-facing gloss where useful.
- Deprecate apparent age for this meaning.
- Preserve apparent maturity and quotations where those are the intended concepts.
- Revise radiometric discussion to distinguish measurement from chronology inference and remove unnecessarily polemical wording.
