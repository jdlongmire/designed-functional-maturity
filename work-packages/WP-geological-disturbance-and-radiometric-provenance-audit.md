# Geological Disturbance and Radiometric Provenance Audit

**Programme:** Designed Functional Maturity (DFM)  
**Date:** 2026-10-25  
**Status:** Research protocol / exploratory; no empirical result claimed.  
**Dependency:** WP-radiometric-provenance-classification-and-tests.md; WP-radiogenic-daughter-provenance-adjudication.md.

## Research question
To what extent do terrestrial recycling, metamorphism, fluid alteration, melting, erosion, impact, and radiation damage erase primordial information, reset isotopic systems, or preserve interpretable event histories? How can DFM distinguish those outcomes without assigning chronology on the basis of agreement or disagreement with its prior commitments?

## Central distinction
1. **Primordial record loss:** The original terrestrial crust is not preserved as an intact representative archive; the Earth-formation estimate relies significantly on meteorite and terrestrial Pb systematics.
2. **Sample-specific disturbance:** Individual minerals can undergo Pb loss, U gain, inherited-core mixing, recrystallization, diffusion, and alteration, potentially producing discordance.
3. **Preserved process record:** Disturbance can also generate diagnostic zoning, discordia, nanoscopic Pb reservoirs, and other signatures that constrain actual events. Alteration does not automatically invalidate an age.
4. **Independent event order:** Field contacts, crosscutting relationships, stratigraphic superposition, metamorphic overprints, and impact structures constrain sequence apart from any one radiometric date.

## Disturbance mechanism matrix

| Mechanism | Material consequence | Possible isotope effect | Independent evidence to collect | DFM historical-integrity requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subduction and crustal recycling | Original rocks consumed or reworked | Complete loss of some primordial records; surviving inherited components possible | Regional geology, provenance, mineral inclusions | Distinguish missing record from invalid existing measurement |
| Partial melting / magmatism | New minerals and inherited remnants | New crystallization clocks, inheritance, partial reset | Intrusion contacts, zoning, geochemistry | Model real crystallization and inheritance |
| Regional/contact metamorphism | Recrystallization, diffusion, new growth | Partial Pb loss, reset of some systems, retention of others | Mineral assemblages, P–T paths, closure temperatures | Model actual metamorphic process |
| Hydrothermal alteration | Fluid-mediated dissolution and reprecipitation | Pb loss, U gain, mixed domains, discordance | Fluid inclusions, oxygen isotopes, textures | Model fluid pathway and mass balance |
| Erosion / sediment recycling | Detrital transport and deposition | Zircon source age differs from host sediment deposition | Sedimentary context, rounding, provenance, ash beds | Do not equate detrital grain age with deposition |
| Impact and deformation | Shock, damage, heating, melt | Local resetting or preserved inherited components | Shock features, impact melt, structures | Identify actual impact or mark unresolved |
| Radiation damage / annealing | Defects and altered diffusion | Susceptibility to Pb loss, heterogeneous retention | Microstructure, alpha damage, nanoscale Pb mapping | Model damage accumulation and healing |

## Provenance and confidence labels
Use orthogonal labels, not a single 'reliable/unreliable' verdict.

- **Record state:** intact / partly disturbed / extensively reworked / unresolved.
- **Event attribution:** primordial candidate / actual deployment process / post-deployment event / mixed / unknown.
- **Dating interpretation:** concordant; discordant with constrained event model; discordant unresolved; known-age calibration; model-dependent age.
- **Independent controls:** strong / moderate / weak / absent, with named evidence.
- **DFM model status:** derived / prospective prediction / accommodation / unresolved / failed.

Never infer 'initialized' from an old numerical date alone, and never infer 'unreliable' from evidence of disturbance alone.

## Audit phases

### Phase 1. Foundational age-of-Earth dependency graph
Separate: (a) meteorite Pb isotope baseline; (b) terrestrial lead ore information; (c) earliest terrestrial rock/mineral lower-bound constraints; (d) lunar corroboration. Distinguish shared assumptions and genuinely independent evidence. Avoid describing 4.54 Ga as a direct radiometric date of an intact original Earth rock.

### Phase 2. Representative terrestrial chronometer set
Preselect case studies from ancient zircon, metamorphic zircon, dated volcanic ash, intrusion contacts, and disturbed/undisturbed known-age samples. For each record: measured isotope systems, uncertainty, mineral texture, field order, inferred closure/reset, and whether an independent process model exists.

### Phase 3. Disturbance physics
For each specimen, construct forward models of diffusion, lead loss, mixing, recrystallization and/or growth with explicit initial and boundary conditions. Quantify whether disturbance would predict concordance, discordance, or recognizable multi-domain structure. Use physically reasonable parameter ranges rather than arbitrary specimen-specific adjustments.

### Phase 4. DFM provenance alternatives
Evaluate (A) systemic functional maturity, (B) actual generative deployment, and (C) common coherent isotopic initialization, with possible constrained hybrids. Require each to predict more than an age number: zoning, covariance, texture, spatial distributions, and event ordering.

### Phase 5. Holdout and adverse cases
Freeze rules before evaluating withheld samples. Include: a concordant ancient zircon; a discordant zircon with independently constrained metamorphism; a dated volcanic ash within a stratigraphic sequence; and a known-age calibration sample. Explicitly document observations that DFM fails to reproduce.

## Discriminating questions and failure criteria

**D-01:** Does geological recycling explain why no intact primordial Earth rock is available? Yes, it is a conventional geological explanation, not a uniquely DFM prediction.

**D-02:** Does disturbance necessarily destroy all meaningful isotope chronology? No. This claim is falsified by samples whose distinct events are independently constrained and reproduced by appropriate chronometers.

**D-03:** Can a single constrained DFM initial-state model predict isotope distributions across different minerals and bodies without using target inferred ages? Failure if each sample requires a freely tuned initial daughter ratio.

**D-04:** Can a proposed Flood-related or other rapid terrestrial disturbance account for measured diffusion, zoning, isotope loss and retention across minerals with different thermal sensitivities? Failure if physical budgets, field relationships, or required temperature histories contradict the mechanism.

**D-05:** Does the provenance rule independently locate a creation boundary? Failure if the boundary is assigned retrospectively wherever a conventional age conflicts with the preferred chronology.

**D-06:** Can DFM preserve real event traces without fictionalizing crystallization, impacts, metamorphism, and sedimentation? Failure if purportedly contingent traces lack actual causal processes.

## Research status and caveat
The terrestrial primordial archive is substantially lost or reworked, but surviving terrestrial minerals contain extensive deep-time isotopic evidence. Standard geochronology recognizes disturbance and frequently uses it to reconstruct multiple episodes. This work package does not establish that conventional chronology is wrong or that DFM has explained the measurements. It defines a constrained way to test that possibility.
