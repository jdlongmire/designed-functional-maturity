# WP-002: Retrodiction, Initialization, and Radiogenic Inference

**Status:** In progress  
**Parent:** Designed Functional Maturity (DFM) Research Programme  
**Target:** Formal framework plus controlled revision inputs for the DFM research-programme paper

## 1. Purpose

Establish the epistemic and physical rules governing reconstruction of historical states from present observations, with radiogenic inference as the first empirical application.

The work package will formalize the distinction among observation, model-conditioned retrodiction, and actual history; define constrained initialization under DFM; prevent ad hoc use of mature initial conditions; and develop a radiogenic research track that does not assume accelerated nuclear decay merely to compress conventional retrodictive chronology.

## 2. Core epistemic model

Define:

```text
O_t = observed state at time t
M   = dynamical/model structure
A   = auxiliary assumptions
B   = boundary conditions
R   = retrodictively reconstructed trajectory
H   = actual historical trajectory
```

Then:

```text
R = R(O_t | M, A, B)
```

The existence of a valid reconstruction does not by itself establish:

```text
R = H
```

Identification of the reconstructed trajectory with actual history requires additional warrant establishing historical provenance.

Working formulation:

```text
Observed State + Model + Auxiliaries + Boundary Conditions -> Retrodiction
Retrodiction != Actual History by logical necessity
```

This distinction is not a rejection of retrodictive science. It specifies the epistemic content of retrodiction.

## 3. Age terminology

Discontinue **apparent age** as technical terminology.

- **Historical age (T_H):** actual elapsed duration since the relevant event or origin.
- **Retrodictive age (T_R):** elapsed duration reconstructed by applying model M, auxiliaries A, and boundary conditions B to observed state O_t.
- **Extrapolated age:** acceptable public-facing terminology where useful, subordinate to the formal term *retrodictive age*.

Formally:

```text
T_R = T(O_t | M, A, B)
```

The proposition:

```text
T_R = T_H
```

is a historical inference requiring justification.

## 4. Initialization framework

Let the initialized creation state be represented as:

```text
S_0 = F + I_s
```

where:

- **F** = state required for immediate functional operation.
- **I_s** = state-consistent initialization information.

Observed features are provisionally classified as:

1. **Functional initialization:** features required for immediate operation of the created system.
2. **State-consistent initialization information:** features accompanying the initialized state because physical coherence requires them or because they form part of the specified initial condition.
3. **Historical traces:** features generated by actual events occurring after initialization.

This classification must be independently constrained. It is not a mechanism for assigning inconvenient observations to initialization.

## 5. No-smuggling rule

Neither DFM nor a competing programme may describe an inferred historical trajectory as an observation.

Isotope concentrations and ratios, heat flow, mineral structure, spectra, sequences, and retention characteristics are observations. Ages and antecedent trajectories derived from them are inferences.

DFM likewise may not classify a feature as initialization information merely because its conventional historical interpretation conflicts with DFM chronology.

## 6. No-free-lunch rule

DFM initialization parameters must be independently constrained.

```text
T_R != T_H
```

does not license:

```text
S_0 = whatever initial state produces the desired chronology
```

A proposed initial state must satisfy independent constraints from physical coherence, chemistry, system functionality, canonical boundary conditions, and other observations. Adjustments that exist only to preserve the preferred chronology count against the programme under its Lakatosian criteria.

## 7. Radiogenic inference architecture

Separate four hypotheses that must not be conflated:

```text
H_I = initial radiogenic inventory
H_D = ordinary radioactive decay
H_G = subsequent geochemical history
H_A = accelerated or variable nuclear decay
```

The default DFM research model is:

```text
H_I + H_D + H_G
```

Accelerated decay, `H_A`, is neither part of the DFM hard core nor required by this work package.

## 8. Ordinary-decay default

DFM adopts experimentally measured decay behavior prospectively from its historical boundary unless independent evidence establishes otherwise:

```text
lambda_DFM = lambda_observed
```

A discrepancy between retrodictive and historical chronology is not itself evidence for accelerated decay:

```text
T_R >> T_H
```

does not entail:

```text
lambda_past >> lambda_observed
```

A physical rate must not be altered merely to compress a retrodictive chronology.

## 9. RATE firewall

Accelerated nuclear decay receives no presumption from a short chronology.

If future observations independently require variable decay, it may be investigated as a separate auxiliary hypothesis. It then inherits the full physical burden of the proposed nuclear energy release, including thermal consequences and cross-isotope consistency.

The DFM programme will not inherit accelerated-decay heat-disposal problems merely to reproduce a conventional retrodictive inventory within a short chronology.

## 10. Primary radiogenic research question

Given present isotopic measurements:

1. Which components of the inferred chronology derive directly from observation?
2. Which derive from experimentally established nuclear behavior?
3. Which depend on initial-state assumptions?
4. Which depend on subsequent geological and geochemical history?
5. Which observations independently constrain each component?

Working decomposition:

```text
O_iso + M_decay + B_initial + G_history -> T_R
```

Each term must be independently interrogated.

## 11. Known-age calibration track

Known-age geological events are high-value test cases because historical age can be constrained independently of the radiometric reconstruction.

Compare:

```text
T_R(O | M, A, B)
```

against independently constrained:

```text
T_H
```

Mount St. Helens is to be treated as one calibration/anomaly case, not as a general proof against radiometric dating. The research track will evaluate the actual samples, analytical methods, initial-state assumptions, inherited or excess daughter products, mineral heterogeneity, analytical limits, and published criticisms.

The track should include mainstream known-age lava studies and other calibration cases so that conclusions do not depend on a single contested example.

## 12. Adversarial requirement

The work package must investigate the methods modern geochronology uses to detect, constrain, or mitigate initialization and system-history problems, including where applicable:

- isochron methods;
- Ar-Ar plateau and inverse-isochron methods;
- mineral separation;
- closure temperatures;
- concordia/discordia analysis;
- cross-isotope concordance;
- inherited components;
- open-system behavior;
- diffusion;
- mixing;
- metamorphic resetting;
- analytical detection limits.

The research question is not whether geochronology recognizes such complications. The question is how much historical information remains uniquely recoverable after they are modeled and independently constrained.

## 13. Falsification pressure

DFM must actively seek observations difficult for its initialization model.

Cross-system concordance is a principal test. If independently initialized isotope systems repeatedly converge on the same retrodictive chronology, DFM cannot assign each system an arbitrary initial state.

A successful DFM account must show why:

```text
T_R1 ~= T_R2 ~= T_R3 ...
```

follows from a single coherent initialization plus subsequent history.

Failure to provide a common account counts against the proposed auxiliary rather than licensing additional unconstrained initialization parameters.

## 14. Workstreams

### WS-1: Formal epistemology
Formalize observation, model, auxiliary, boundary condition, retrodiction, provenance, retrodictive age, and historical age.

### WS-2: Initialization-state constraints
Develop the functional-initialization and state-consistent-information rules, including explicit no-smuggling and no-free-lunch tests.

### WS-3: Radiogenic/isotopic case study
Build an evidence ledger separating measured isotope state, nuclear physics, initial-condition assumptions, geochemical history, and inferred chronology. Include known-age calibration cases.

### WS-4: Adversarial testing
Identify observations and methods capable of discriminating DFM initialization from ordinary deep-time provenance. Give special attention to cross-system concordance and independently constrained system histories.

## 16. Acceptance criteria

This WP is ready to feed the flagship DFM paper when:

- the distinction between observation, retrodiction, and actual history is formally explicit;
- initialization categories have independent constraints;
- the no-smuggling and no-free-lunch rules are operational rather than rhetorical;
- ordinary measured decay is the default prospective physics;
- accelerated decay is explicitly non-required and separately burdened;
- at least one known-age calibration dataset has been evaluated from primary technical sources;
- modern geochronological controls have been represented fairly;
- cross-system concordance has been treated as a genuine discriminating burden;
- proposed revisions do not add a new auxiliary merely to protect chronology.

## 17. Governing principle

```text
DFM does not need faster clocks.
It needs a rigorous account of what the clocks are measuring,
what their models infer, and what warrants identifying that inference with actual history.
```
