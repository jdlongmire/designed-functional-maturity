## Accepted conceptual propositions
1. **Terrestrial observational centrality:** Observations are made from Earth and show approximate large-scale isotropy and a redshift-distance relation. These alone do not identify a unique physical center. The author regards observational symmetry combined with Genesis deployment chronology as motivation to investigate terrestrial centrality.
2. **Terrestrial deployment/chronological centrality:** The six days are Earth-referenced terrestrial days. Day 4 cosmological deployment may involve asynchronous generative runtime relative to the terrestrial clock.
3. **Terrestrial Centrality Hypothesis (TCH):** Earth is hypothesized to lie approximately at the coincident centers of global spatial symmetry, large-scale matter distribution, and a physically distinguished expansion structure. Centrality is proposed as both an original deployment condition and an enduring property. Earth's orbital/peculiar motion does not require exact static coincidence. A coordinate origin is not a physical center; homogeneous FLRW expansion has no unique physical center. A specific non-FLRW or other physically distinguished geometry must be supplied and tested.
4. **Day 4 expansion initiation:** The miraculous Day 4 deployment itself initiates the expansion continuing today, rather than merely initializing a universe already expanding from an earlier independent event.
5. **Normative stabilization:** By the end of Day 4, miraculous generative deployment ceases and the cosmic state satisfies the constraints for continuing ordinary physical evolution. Stabilization does not imply static matter or cessation of expansion.
6. **Hybrid Post-Deployment Cosmological Residuals (PDCR):** Explore persistent, transient, and inherited features of the stabilized state. These are candidate explanatory classes, not confirmed identifications.
