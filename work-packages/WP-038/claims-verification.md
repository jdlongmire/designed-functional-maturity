# WP-038 Claims Register Verification (Slice 2)

Date: 2026-11-15. Scope: claims C-1 to C-3 from creationist-literature-map.md, checked against primary sources. Secondary sources were used only as leads; every verdict below rests on a primary text or dataset identified by name.

## C-1: "Counts of truly shared human-chimpanzee ERVs are overstated"

**The two circulating figures, traced to source:**
- **205** is the HERV-W family count. Grandi and colleagues catalogued 211 HERV-W loci in the human genome and found 205 of them present at the orthologous position in chimpanzee (Grandi et al., analysis of ERV-W sequences in Catarrhini primate genomes, BMC Evolutionary Biology 2018; data table at PMC5775608). It is a within-family figure: 205 of 211, about 97 percent shared, for one ERV family.
- **336** is a filtered study sample. "Orthologous endogenous retroviruses exhibit directional selection since the chimp-human split" (Retrovirology 2015, PMC4477479) identified 336 chimp-human orthologous pairs of full-length ERVs after deliberately filtering for full-length, alignable proviruses. Solo LTRs, which are roughly 90 percent of ERV-derived loci, were excluded by design. The paper was measuring divergence rates, not counting total shared ERVs.

**Genome-wide primary figures:**
- Human genome (IHGSC, Nature 2001): about 203,000 ERV insertions (classes I-III).
- Chimpanzee genome (Chimpanzee Sequencing and Analysis Consortium, Nature 2005, Table 2): 82 human-specific and 279 chimpanzee-specific ERV insertions; all remaining human ERVs are at orthologous positions in chimpanzee.
- Polavarapu, Bowen and McDonald (Genome Biology 2006): 425 full-length chimpanzee ERVs in 42 families, 40 families with human orthologs; fewer than 0.1 percent of the elements they surveyed were lineage-specific.

**Verdict: not sustained as stated.** Both small numbers are real but neither is a genome-wide count of shared ERVs; one is a single-family count and the other is a filtered full-length sample. Cited as totals, they misrepresent their sources. No primary Tomkins or Jeanson text performing a genome-wide recount was found; their published ERV arguments are the functional/design argument (Tomkins, Acts & Facts 2015; ICR news 2016), the exogenization proposal (Tomkins 2015, citing Borger 2009), and the claim that nested hierarchies are non-discriminating evidence (Jeanson and Tomkins, in Searching for Adam, Master Books 2016). The benchmark recount under the WP-038 orthology rule remains the programme's own check and proceeds regardless.

**Related factual correction.** Tomkins (Acts & Facts 2015) asserts that stable germline integration of retroviruses "has never been documented". That is incorrect: Tarlinton, Meers and Young (Nature 2006, 442: 79-81) documented the koala retrovirus in the act of endogenizing, present in northern koala germlines and absent from isolated southern populations, an invasion dated to within roughly the last 100 to 200 years (see also Stoye, Genome Biology 2006). Germline endogenization is an observed process, not only an inferred one.

## C-2: "Supposedly shared loci show flanking differences inconsistent with a single ancestral integration"

**Verdict: unverified as a specific claim; the underlying question is benchmark-testable.** No primary Tomkins or Jeanson publication presenting a locus-level flanking-sequence dataset was located. The claim circulates in summary form without an accession-level table behind it. Two standing facts frame the test: the 2015 Retrovirology orthology study established its 336 pairs using flanking DNA, so flanking comparison is standard practice and not a creationist innovation; and post-integration flanking change (indels, deletions, rearrangements) is expected under C0 as well, which is why the literature map pre-registered the classification rule (post-integration change versus integration-structure difference). The benchmark's class 1 locus extraction will produce the first dataset in this programme capable of confirming or refuting C-2 in specific form.

## C-3: "ERV sequence similarity is lower than surrounding-genome similarity, counting against common ancestry"

**Verdict: the observation is real and published by mainstream workers; the inference does not discriminate.** The 2015 Retrovirology study found that orthologous ERVs have diverged faster than neighbouring selfish DNA since the chimp-human split, with divergence correlating with LTR length and with evidence of directional selection, strongest in the HERV-H family. Faster ERV divergence is therefore a mainstream result, explained within C0 by relaxed constraint on mostly non-functional loci plus selection on exapted elements. As pre-registered in the register, raw similarity is not the discriminating quantity between C0 and the D models; the pattern of shared derived marks at orthologous loci is. C-3 is closed as non-discriminating.
