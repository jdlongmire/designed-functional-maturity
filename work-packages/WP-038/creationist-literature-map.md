# WP-038 Creationist-Literature Map

Source: considerations summary supplied by JD, 2026-11-15, surveying how Answers in Genesis, the Institute for Creation Research and Creation Ministries International address endogenous retroviruses. This document maps each argument family onto the WP-038 model set, tests it against the Historical Integrity Constraint and AHPP, and records what the programme must do with it. It is a map, not a verdict: claims marked unverified have not been checked against primary texts in this programme yet.

## 1. Functional design and common design

**The argument.** Many ERV-derived sequences are functional: promoters, enhancers, regulators of development and immunity, and co-opted genes such as the syncytins in placental formation. Therefore ERVs are designed elements, and shared functional ERVs across humans and primates reflect common design rather than common ancestry. "Created heterozygosity" is invoked for initialized diversity.

**Mapping.** This is the positive case behind D1's initialization list, and the programme accepts its legitimate core: a sequence's present function is real evidence about its present role, and any ERV-derived element with an independent functional warrant is a lawful initialization candidate under D1, at the cost of one declared initialization choice per element.

**Where it does not reach.** Function answers "what does it do now", not "how did it get there". Three gaps must be recorded honestly:
- Co-option is a documented mainstream mechanism, not an embarrassment to it: a viral gene captured for host use (syncytin is the standard example) is evidence that an endogenization event occurred, and the capture explanation requires the event. Function therefore cannot, by itself, distinguish design-initialization from capture-after-infection.
- The syncytin pattern cuts against a single common-design reading: different mammalian lineages carry different, unrelated ERV families co-opted for the same placental function (syncytin-1 and -2 in primates, syncytin-A and -B in rodents, others elsewhere). Common design must explain why the same function was implemented from different viral raw material in different lineages; the capture model predicts exactly that pattern. D1 is charged with this as a named burden.
- Function does not address the event-diagnostic evidence class at all: matched integration boundaries, target-site duplications, LTR divergence, and shared disabling mutations in non-functional loci. A designed functional element does not need a pair of LTRs that diverge like a molecular clock, nor a shared inactivating deletion at the same nucleotide in two species. Under Historical Integrity, initialized function is lawful; initialized event traces are not. The functional argument therefore covers at most the functional subset of loci, and the benchmark scores functional and non-functional loci separately so this scope limit is visible in the results rather than blurred.

**Programme action.** Benchmark locus records carry a functional-annotation field (already in the schema); scoring is reported split by functional status. D1's initialization list may only claim loci with an independent functional warrant recorded in advance.

## 2. Exogenization (host-to-virus origin)

**The argument.** ERVs were created as beneficial mobile regulatory elements; after the Fall, some escaped cellular control and became exogenous infectious retroviruses. The direction of derivation runs host-to-virus, reversing the standard account.

**Mapping.** This is not covered by D1 as formalized, because D1's initialized elements are static functional structures, whereas exogenization posits created elements with transposition machinery and a subsequent degeneration history. It is added to the formalization as candidate model D4, with its own generative statement, degrees of freedom and failure modes (see model-formalization.md). Its distinctive, testable content:
- If exogenous retroviruses derive from created endogenous elements, exogenous viral phylogenies should root within or beside endogenous element diversity, and the oldest endogenous copies should show the least degradation consistent with their post-Fall degeneration timeline.
- Created mobile elements should show design coherence as a family (regulatory targeting, controlled transposition), with infectious escape as the derived, degraded state.
- D4 inherits D1's Historical Integrity boundary in full: LTR divergence, shared disabling mutations and orthologous boundary structure at shared loci are event traces of transposition and infection events, and D4 must supply real events for them inside its own timeline. Its compressed post-Fall timeline makes the required event and mutation counts a scored quantity, not a detail: the benchmark's per-locus LTR divergence and shared-mark data are exactly the quantities D4 must fit with far fewer generations than C0 allows itself. AHPP charges both sides symmetrically for the rates they assume.

**Programme action.** D4 added as a candidate model pending JD/GPT review. The benchmark's LTR-divergence fields serve as its primary severe test.

## 3. Integration site preferences

**The argument.** Retroviral integration is non-random (motifs, open chromatin, hotspots), so independent insertions at the same locus in humans and apes are not improbable.

**Mapping.** This is D3, already formalized. The literature summary states the argument at the strength the creationist literature gives it; the formalization states the quantitative version the argument must survive: targeting biases are measured distributions, they act on integration sites, and the joint pattern to be explained includes exact boundary, orientation, TSD, and shared post-integration mutations, on which targeting has no purchase. Yohn et al. 2005 supplies the empirical base rate: independent integrations are common and almost never orthologous (about 95.8 percent non-orthologous between chimpanzee and gorilla), so the existence of hotspots is not in dispute; their sufficiency for the shared-locus pattern is the question, and it is a question with a measurable answer.

**Programme action.** None new; D3 calibration proceeds from the experimental targeting literature (register entries 2 and 3) as designed.

## 4. Re-evaluating the shared-ERV data (Tomkins, Jeanson)

**The argument.** Named creationist geneticists argue that counts of truly shared human-chimpanzee ERVs are overstated, and that flanking-sequence examination shows structural differences inconsistent with single ancestral integrations.

**Mapping.** This is an empirical claim family about the benchmark's own subject matter, and it is the kind of claim this programme exists to settle at accession level. It enters the programme as a claims register (below), not as an assumption in either direction. Two cautions are recorded with it: the claims must be taken from the primary creationist papers, not from summaries by either side; and any mainstream rebuttals must likewise be checked against the underlying locus data rather than quoted. The benchmark's both-flanks-plus-TSD orthology rule is precisely the instrument these claims call for, which makes this family the most directly testable of the four.

**Claims register (all entries unverified at filing; verification is slice 2 work).**
- C-1: The count of genuinely orthologous human-chimpanzee ERV loci is substantially lower than commonly cited figures (figures in circulation include 205 and 336 from different sources and definitions). Verify: trace each figure to its primary table, its orthology definition and its assembly builds; recount under the benchmark's orthology rule.
- C-2: Supposedly shared loci show flanking-sequence differences (deletions, structural variation) inconsistent with a single ancestral integration. Verify: per-locus flank comparison on the class 1 locus set; classify differences as post-integration change (compatible with C0 and expected by it) versus integration-structure difference (genuinely discriminating), with the classification rule stated in advance.
- C-3: Overall ERV sequence similarity between human and chimpanzee is lower than the similarity of the surrounding genome, which is argued to count against common ancestry. Verify: identify the primary source and its method; note in advance that ERV sequences under relaxed constraint are expected to diverge faster than constrained genomic regions under C0 as well, so the discriminating quantity is the pattern of shared derived marks, not raw similarity.
