# Daughter-Product Provenance: Measurement versus Historical Attribution

**DFM research work package | 2026-10-25 | Exploratory, not ratified**

## Question
How much of a measured stable daughter isotope inventory is directly established to derive from in-situ decay, and how much is assigned to decay by an inference model? This is a question of evidentiary provenance, **not** a claim that radiometric dating is arbitrary or that radiogenic production is unobserved.

## Distinctions
1. **Measured:** current isotope ratios, elemental concentrations, mineral textures and distributions.
2. **Experimentally established:** nuclear transformation pathways, branching ratios, decay constants under tested conditions.
3. **Model-inferred:** the initial nonradiogenic/common component, subsequent in-situ radiogenic ingrowth, open-system transfers, crystallization/closure ages and event sequences.
4. **Independent corroboration:** petrography, stratigraphy, cross-cutting relationships, diffusion/closure models, concordance among multiple clocks.

An isotope's nuclear identity alone does not carry an intrinsic label indicating its individual provenance. However, multivariate isotope patterns and geological context may strongly constrain provenance.

## Core equations
For a closed system with one parent P and daughter D, measured at elapsed time t:
P(t)=P0 exp(-lambda t)
D(t)=D0+P0(1-exp(-lambda t))
Equivalently, D(t)=D0+P(t)(exp(lambda t)-1).
Unknown D0 prevents a unique time inference from a single pair without additional constraints. Isochrons and common-daughter isotope corrections are among established methods for constraining D0. This is a conventional recognition, not a DFM discovery.

For uranium-lead systems:
206Pb = 206Pb_initial + 238U_now (exp(lambda238 t)-1) + transfers
207Pb = 207Pb_initial + 235U_now (exp(lambda235 t)-1) + transfers
The shorthand 'transfers' is schematic and must be replaced with explicit mass balance in a quantitative model. Initial Pb is conventionally estimated or corrected, often using nonradiogenic 204Pb, mineral context, and regression.

## Comparative evidence matrix
| Evidence | What is observed | Conventional inference | DFM-specific burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| 206Pb, 207Pb in zircon | Ratios and mineral chemistry | U-series daughter ingrowth after zircon crystallization, correcting initial common Pb | Quantify any initialized fraction without arbitrary tuning |
| U-Pb concordance | Two independent U decay-chain ratios agree | Shared closed-system crystallization history | Derive correlated initial inventories or actual events explaining both clocks |
| U-Pb discordance | Deviations from concordia and mineral zonation | Lead loss, inherited domains, resetting or mixing as supported | Explain field and microtextural correlations with real events |
| Rb-Sr isochron | Correlated isotope ratios across samples | Common initial 87Sr/86Sr and subsequent ingrowth | Predict slope/intercept and exclude initial mixing artifacts with independent checks |
| K-Ar / Ar-Ar | K and argon isotopes, release spectra | Accumulation, trapped/excess argon, loss, thermal history | Model initial/trapped Ar and measured step-heating patterns |
| Independent geology | Cross-cutting, stratigraphy, mineral growth and alteration | Actual ordered geological processes | Respect historical integrity; cannot initialize fictitious event traces |

## Strongest counterargument
The conventional account is not merely 'daughter exists therefore time passed'. Zircon commonly incorporates U but little initial Pb, making its daughter enrichment especially informative; two uranium chains and common-Pb corrections produce strong internal checks. Modern reporting standards explicitly require isotope-ratio covariance and common-Pb treatment. A proposed initialized inventory that reproduces such patterns by unconstrained fitting is not an explanatory competitor.

## DFM candidate models
- M0: Conventional crystallization, decay and geological evolution.
- M1: Coherently constituted initial parent/daughter reservoirs plus ordinary subsequent decay.
- M2: Real extraordinary generative processes plus ordinary subsequent decay, with physical energy accounting.
- M3: Constrained hybrid including subsequent open-system geological events.

Do not equate 'possible initialized daughters' with demonstrated initial daughters. Require a common generator with independently fixed parameters, cross-mineral and cross-isotope holdouts, and explicit event-provenance tests.

## Priority empirical protocol
1. Select one well-characterized zircon U-Pb dataset reporting raw or corrected isotope ratios, common-Pb treatment, mineral zoning and analytical covariance.
2. Separate direct measurements from corrections, closure assumptions, and inferred ages.
3. Reproduce the conventional concordia and uncertainties first.
4. Specify M1 initialization rules **before** fitting held-out crystals.
5. Fit one subset and predict held-out 206Pb/238U, 207Pb/235U and 207Pb/206Pb ratios.
6. Test geological and textural event coherence and parameter economy against M0.
7. Publish unresolved residuals and explicit failure criteria.
