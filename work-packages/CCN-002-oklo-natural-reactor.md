# CCN-002 — Oklo Natural Fission Reactor

**Status:** Active severe-test baseline  
**Region:** II — Historical Development  
**Track:** A and conventional comparator, scored separately  
**Linked work:** WP-026, WP-002, WP-008, WP-012, WP-025, CCN-001

## 1. Claim under test

What historical conclusions are warranted by the coupled nuclear, isotopic, mineralogical, and geological evidence at Oklo, and can DFM reproduce that network under an independently specified compressed history without importing conventional elapsed time?

## 2. O -> L -> R -> H decomposition

### O — Observation

Measured present-state quantities include:

- anomalous/depleted U-235 relative to ordinary natural uranium;
- isotope compositions of U and multiple fission-product / neutron-capture elements;
- spatial distributions of fissiogenic products across reactor zones and host minerals;
- mineralogical hosts and alteration textures;
- U-Pb, Rb-Sr and related isotope ratios;
- signatures attributable to neutron irradiation and nucleogenic Pu.

### L — Operational physics

Relevant operational laws include:

- radioactive decay of U-235 and U-238 with distinct decay constants;
- neutron-induced fission and capture cross sections;
- neutron moderation and criticality physics;
- fission yields;
- diffusion, dissolution, precipitation, mineral partitioning and fluid transport.

DFM accepts ordinary measured prospective nuclear physics by default.

### R — Reconstruction

The conventional reconstruction infers:

- sustained natural criticality;
- neutron fluence and spectrum;
- reactor composition and moderation conditions;
- burnup and fission contributions;
- selective post-fission transport/retention;
- an ancient reactor event conventionally near 2 Ga.

These are reconstructions from the present state under specified physics and geological auxiliaries.

### H — Historical interpretation

The historical claim identifies the reconstructed trajectory and duration with the actual past history of the deposit.

DFM does not deny the inferential category. It must nevertheless confront the unusually dense cross-constraint network supporting that reconstruction.

## 3. Key convergence node

The most important severe-test relation is:

```text
present U235/U238
+ measured lambda235 and lambda238
-> reconstructed earlier U235/U238
```

and independently:

```text
ore composition + geometry + moderator + neutron physics
-> U235 abundance required for natural criticality
```

If these converge near the same reconstructed state without circular dependence, that is substantially stronger than a single radiometric clock.

The first quantitative task is to measure the independence and sensitivity of this convergence.

## 4. Transport constraint

Oklo cannot be represented as simply closed or open.

Primary literature reports element-specific behavior: some fission products were strongly retained, others partly or substantially lost, and later alteration/remobilization occurred. Therefore every closure/disturbance claim must specify:

- isotope/element;
- mineral host;
- reactor zone;
- spatial scale;
- proposed mechanism;
- timing relative to criticality;
- independent evidence for that mechanism.

## 6. Separate burden ledger

### Conventional burden

- demonstrate independence of the differential-decay/criticality convergence;
- constrain reactor geometry, water moderation, neutron poisons, temperature and burnup;
- demonstrate rather than assume closure or disturbance for each isotope system;
- distinguish reactor age from ore formation and later alteration;
- propagate model uncertainty into chronological claims.

### DFM burden

- specify an initial U isotopic architecture without conventional-age back-calculation;
- obtain criticality under ordinary nuclear physics;
- generate the measured coupled fission/capture inventory;
- explain selective migration and retention;
- reproduce chronology-sensitive structure without a latent age surrogate;
- supply a discriminator that was not used to tune the model.

Neither burden transfers.

## 8. Current evidential appraisal

**Natural fission:** strongly established.

**Element-specific transport:** strongly established; invalidates both naive universal closure and naive universal disturbance.

**Conventional historical reconstruction:** unusually strong model-conditioned reconstruction because nuclear reactor physics, isotope physics, geochemistry and geological context interact.

**DFM:** no quantitative Oklo model yet. Current status is a Region II debit and severe challenge.

**Numerical Bayes factor:** not assigned. Dependencies and likelihoods are not yet sufficiently quantified.

## 9. High-value discriminator programme

Priority tests:

1. calculate the U-235 fraction required for criticality across defensible RZ parameter ranges;
2. independently calculate the U-235 fraction implied by differential U decay as a function of elapsed time;
3. compare these curves without using reactor age as an input to the criticality calculation;
4. map isotope retention/migration against mineral host and transport mechanism;
5. test whether any DFM initialization generator can hit the coupled target without target-derived parameters;
6. identify observations that a compressed-history Oklo model forbids.

## 10. Current disposition

**Severe Challenge / active.**

Oklo is a particularly strong test because it couples operational reactor physics to historical isotope reconstruction. DFM should attempt the test at full strength. If the current auxiliary fails, record the failure and revise or reject that auxiliary rather than weakening the evidential standard.
