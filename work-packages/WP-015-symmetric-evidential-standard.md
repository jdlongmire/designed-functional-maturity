# WP-015: Symmetric Evidential Standard

**Status:** Executed  
**Type:** Programme epistemology / comparative appraisal  
**Depends on:** WP-014

## Objective

Prevent asymmetric evidential burdens in comparisons between DFM and prevailing historical reconstructions while preserving DFM's commitment to severe testing.

## Canonical principle

> **Symmetric Evidential Standard:** DFM and competing historical programmes must be evaluated under the same epistemic standards. A requirement imposed on DFM cannot be waived for a competing reconstruction merely because that reconstruction represents the prevailing consensus.

This includes equal scrutiny of initial and boundary conditions, unobserved historical states, auxiliary hypotheses, parameter fitting, post-hoc accommodation, explanatory compression, provenance claims, and severe tests.

## Evidential ladder

Both DFM and competitors should distinguish:

```text
compatibility
    < accommodation
    < independent derivation
    < novel prediction
    < successful severe test
```

Consensus status does not itself move a claim upward on this ladder.

## Historical-model symmetry

DFM may not demand direct observation of a competitor's unobserved history while relying on inference for its own. Conversely, DFM may not be required to directly observe supernatural initialization while a competing programme is permitted to infer its unobserved history from present states.

Both must expose:

```text
observations
+ model structure
+ initial/boundary conditions
+ auxiliaries
+ provenance assumptions
-> historical inference
```

## No race to the bottom

> **Weakness, underdetermination, or auxiliary dependence in a competing programme does not constitute positive evidence for DFM.**

The symmetry principle is a comparative-discipline rule, not permission to lower DFM's evidential standard.

## OAR burden-symmetry check

Before accepting an objection as a special burden on DFM, ask:

1. Is the objection a universal epistemic standard?
2. If so, is the same standard applied to the competing reconstruction?
3. What is directly observed on each side?
4. What is inferred?
5. Which initial/boundary conditions are independently constrained?
6. Which auxiliaries are independently motivated versus introduced after the fact?
7. Is the claimed achievement compatibility, accommodation, derivation, prediction, or severe-test success?
8. Does the objection identify a discriminator, or merely an asymmetry in accepted background assumptions?
