# WP-027 — Low-Initial-Salinity Ocean Hypothesis

**Programme:** Designed Functional Maturity (DFM)  
**Status:** Open ancillary hypothesis / research work package  
**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Owner:** James D. (JD) Longmire  
**Epistemic status:** Proposed. Not part of the DFM hard core. Subject to quantitative constraint, severe testing, revision, or rejection.

## 1. Research question

Could the created ocean have begun with a dissolved-salt inventory substantially below the present ocean while still satisfying the functional requirements of the created marine biosphere, with subsequent ordinary, catastrophic, and post-catastrophic geochemical processes producing the present approximately 35 g/kg mean seawater salinity?

This work package treats initial salinity as an **open initialization parameter**, not as a stipulated value and not as a simple chronometer.

## 2. Hypothesis

### H-LIS: Low-Initial-Salinity Hypothesis

At the creation boundary, the ocean was initialized with a substantially lower dissolved-salt inventory than the present ocean, while possessing the ionic composition and chemical conditions required for immediate ecological function. Subsequent salinity developed through historical water-rock interaction, weathering, riverine transport, hydrothermal and volcanic processes, sediment/mineral exchange, and any major geochemical redistribution associated with the Flood and post-Flood recovery.

The hypothesis does **not** require a fresh-water ocean and does not assign a numerical value to initial salinity in advance of constraint analysis.

## 3. DFM placement

H-LIS is an ancillary hypothesis under DFM's initialization-boundary research programme.

It must remain subordinate to:

1. scripturally warranted history;
2. functional maturity;
3. historical-causal priority;
4. explanatory allocation;
5. the prohibition against using initialization as an evasion of testable historical causes.

Low initial salinity therefore cannot be invoked merely because a present-state extrapolation is inconvenient. It must earn explanatory standing through independent functional and geochemical constraints.

## 4. State variables

Let:

- \(S_0\) = initialized ocean salinity;
- \(M_{s,0}\) = initialized dissolved-salt mass;
- \(V_0\) = initialized ocean volume;
- \(F_{in}(t)\) = aggregate salt/ion input flux;
- \(F_{out}(t)\) = aggregate salt/ion removal flux;
- \(V(t)\) = ocean volume through time;
- \(\Delta M_H\) = net pre-Flood historical salt-budget change;
- \(\Delta M_F\) = net Flood-associated salt-budget change;
- \(\Delta M_P\) = net post-Flood salt-budget change.

The first-order reservoir equation is:

\[
\frac{dM_s}{dt}=F_{in}(t)-F_{out}(t)
\]

with:

\[
S(t) \approx \frac{M_s(t)}{V(t)}
\]

and, schematically:

\[
M_{s,present}=M_{s,0}+\Delta M_H+\Delta M_F+\Delta M_P
\]

This supersedes the overly simple formulation in which present salt inventory divided by present riverine input is treated as an ocean-age clock.

## 5. Functional lower bound

DFM requires immediate functional coherence. Day 5 marine organisms therefore provide an independent constraint on \(S_0\).

The research programme must determine:

- minimum and preferred salinity ranges compatible with representative marine taxa;
- ionic-composition requirements independent of bulk salinity;
- osmoregulatory tolerances;
- requirements for carbonate chemistry, pH, calcium, magnesium, sodium, chloride, sulfate, potassium, and trace constituents;
- whether extant physiology can legitimately constrain created-state physiology, and with what uncertainty.

Accordingly:

\[
S_0 > S_{functional\ minimum}
\]

unless positive evidence warrants a different physiological state.

No value of \(S_0\) is admissible merely because it improves a geochemical trajectory. Functional biology constrains the initialization.

## 6. Present observational constraint

The modern ocean is approximately a steady geochemical reservoir in global mean salinity while exhibiting measurable regional redistribution. Instrumental observations show amplification of regional salinity contrasts, including salinification in evaporation-dominated regions and freshening elsewhere, rather than an obvious globally monotonic salt-accumulation trajectory.

This observation does not establish ancient initial salinity. It does establish that present salinity must be modeled as a reservoir with inputs, outputs, redistribution, and changing water volume rather than as a one-directional accumulation clock.

## 7. Candidate input terms

Quantify at minimum:

- continental chemical weathering;
- riverine dissolved load;
- groundwater/submarine groundwater discharge;
- volcanic inputs;
- hydrothermal circulation;
- water-rock interaction;
- atmospheric deposition;
- dissolution of exposed or mobilized mineral reservoirs;
- Flood-specific erosion, fluid-rock interaction, and mobilization where independently warranted.

Ion-specific budgets are required. Bulk "salt" cannot substitute for Na, Cl, Mg, Ca, K, sulfate, bicarbonate, and other chemically distinct reservoirs.

## 8. Candidate removal terms

Quantify at minimum:

- evaporite deposition;
- carbonate and other mineral precipitation;
- sediment burial and pore-water exchange;
- biological uptake and shell/skeleton formation;
- alteration of oceanic crust;
- hydrothermal removal reactions;
- sea-spray export and continental return pathways;
- adsorption and ion exchange;
- post-depositional diagenesis.

Any proposed trajectory must close the mass balance within stated uncertainty.

## 9. Flood coupling

The Flood is not automatically assigned the role of salinity generator. Under Historical-Causal Priority, however, if the Flood possesses independently warranted causal capacity for large-scale erosion, mineral dissolution, hydrothermal circulation, volcanism, sedimentation, ocean-volume change, or fluid-rock exchange, those mechanisms must be tested before appealing to arbitrary initialization adjustments.

Key question:

> Can independently defensible Flood-process models account for a substantial fraction of the difference between a functionally constrained low \(S_0\) and the present oceanic salt inventory?

This is an empirical modeling question, not a programme commitment.

## 10. Competing trajectories

At least four models should be compared:

**M1 — Near-modern initialization:** \(S_0\) approximately modern; subsequent net global change small.

**M2 — Moderate low-salinity initialization:** \(S_0\) materially below modern but within broadly marine biological tolerances; historical and catastrophic fluxes produce the present state.

**M3 — Very-low-salinity initialization:** \(S_0\) near the lowest biologically viable bound; requires large subsequent net salt addition.

**M4 — Freshwater initialization:** \(S_0\) near zero. This is retained as a boundary case and should be rejected if it conflicts with functional maturity or requires unsupported physiological assumptions.

The programme presently gives provisional interest to M2/M3 but assigns no evidential preference until constraints are calculated.

## 11. Severe tests and failure conditions

H-LIS is weakened or rejected if one or more of the following obtain:

1. Functional marine biology establishes a robust lower bound close to modern salinity.
2. Required post-initialization salt addition exceeds plausible accessible mineral reservoirs or fluxes.
3. The required ionic proportions cannot be generated by the proposed processes.
4. The model requires removal/input rates inconsistent with independent geological evidence.
5. Flood coupling requires mechanisms introduced solely to rescue the salinity trajectory.
6. The hypothesis predicts mineralogical, evaporitic, isotopic, or biological signatures contradicted by observations.
7. A near-modern initialization model explains the same evidence with materially fewer auxiliary assumptions.

Conversely, H-LIS gains support if independently constrained functional minima, source reservoirs, geochemical fluxes, and historical processes converge on a coherent low-\(S_0\) trajectory without parameter tuning designed merely to reproduce the endpoint.

## 12. Evidence programme

### Phase A — Modern reservoir baseline

Establish primary-source values and uncertainties for:

- present ocean mass and volume;
- mean salinity and major-ion inventory;
- riverine dissolved fluxes;
- hydrothermal input/removal;
- evaporite and sediment sinks;
- residence times by ion;
- observed global and regional salinity trends.

### Phase B — Functional biological constraint

Build a taxon-stratified salinity-tolerance ledger emphasizing marine organisms relevant to Day 5 functional requirements.

### Phase C — Geological reservoir accounting

Estimate accessible Na, Cl, Mg, Ca, K, sulfate and carbonate reservoirs and the rates/processes by which they can enter or leave seawater.

### Phase D — Flood-process coupling

Parameterize only mechanisms already independently present in the Flood model. Do not add ad hoc processes solely to close the salt budget.

### Phase E — Bayesian/severe-test comparison

Compare M1-M4 using the DFM symmetric evidential standard. Derived consequences of chosen parameters must not be double-counted as independent evidence.

## 13. Predictions and discriminators

Potential discriminators to investigate:

- ion-ratio trajectories rather than bulk salinity alone;
- evaporite volume and distribution;
- fluid-inclusion paleosalinity claims and their model dependence;
- halite/brine inclusion chemistry;
- carbonate-system constraints;
- boron, chlorine, strontium, sulfur and oxygen isotope systems where relevant;
- biological salinity tolerance and inferred ancestral constraints;
- sedimentary and hydrothermal sink/source capacities;
- whether independent reconstructions actually require approximately modern Archean/Proterozoic seawater salinity or permit broad ranges.

Paleosalinity proxies must be evaluated for initialization assumptions and inverse-model dependence. A proxy-derived ancient salinity is not automatically a direct measurement.

## 14. Guardrails

- Do not use present fluxes as timeless constants without justification.
- Do not infer \(S_0\) from the desired chronology.
- Do not assume all marine organisms had modern physiology without testing the inference.
- Do not alter physiological requirements merely to permit a preferred \(S_0\).
- Do not treat regional modern salinity trends as global salt-inventory trends.
- Do not collapse ionic composition into a single bulk-salinity variable when chemistry matters.
- Do not use initialization where a scripturally warranted historical process has plausible causal capacity without testing that process first.
- Report failed mass balance openly.

## 15. Immediate deliverables

1. Primary-source salinity and major-ion baseline.
2. Ocean salt mass-balance ledger with uncertainty ranges.
3. Marine functional-minimum salinity review.
4. Flood-coupled source/sink matrix.
5. Four-model quantitative comparison.
6. Severe-test register.
7. Decision: retain, revise, demote, or reject H-LIS.

## 16. Provisional conclusion

The low-initial-salinity proposal is scientifically interesting within DFM precisely because it is constrainable. DFM does not need the primordial ocean to possess modern salinity merely because the present ocean does. It does require the initialized ocean to be functionally adequate.

The central research problem is therefore:

> **How low could the initialized ocean's salinity have been while sustaining a functionally mature marine biosphere, and can independently warranted historical processes close the mass balance from that state to the present ocean?**

Until that question is quantitatively answered, \(S_0\) remains an open parameter.
