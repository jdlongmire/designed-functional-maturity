# WP-030: Minimum Required Cosmic Process Depth

**Status:** Active severe-test programme
**Parents:** WP-028, WP-029
**Trigger:** Repeated failure of simple physical realizations of the maximal synchronized-lapse target

## Research question

What minimum genuine cosmic process history is required by the coupled observations, once we distinguish:

1. directly evidenced causal/process structure;
2. elapsed duration inferred through a runtime model;
3. coherently initialized actuality;
4. genuine extraordinary deployment history;
5. ordinary post-commissioning runtime?

This work package does **not** assume that the answer is short. It tests whether the conventional ~13.8 Gyr thermal history is independently required as accumulated local proper time, or whether some of that duration is a model-mediated reconstruction while the underlying causal structure remains real.

## Competing allocations

### A. Full-thermal-history execution

    initialized hot-state boundary
      -> ~13.8 Gyr cosmic proper-time history
      -> synchronization
      -> ordinary runtime.

Strength: inherits the complete standard correlated history.

Burden: requires the extreme Day-4 Earth/cosmos proper-time hierarchy whose simple metric, CMC, dynamical-foliation, attractor, and junction realizations have so far failed to derive economically.

### B. Minimum-required-process-depth deployment

    coherent initialized actuality
      + genuinely executed coupled process histories
      + extraordinary asynchronous deployment where required
      -> synchronization
      -> ordinary runtime.

Strength: does not require conventional runtime duration unless the evidence actually demands it.

Burden: must provide a principled allocation and preserve every coupled causal invariant. It may not initialize observations independently merely to shorten the chronology.

## Evidence classes

### E1 Direct process/coupling evidence

Observations that strongly support a real causal sequence or common event network.

Examples:

- SN 1987A neutrino burst + optical transient;
- radioactive-line evolution;
- ejecta expansion and shock/ring interaction;
- remnant/dust evolution;
- Type Ia light-curve temporal structure;
- intervening absorption along real sightlines.

Default allocation: genuine executed history unless a contrary model is independently warranted.

### E2 Physical-clock/model evidence

Elapsed duration inferred from tested local process physics, including:

- stellar evolution;
- radioactive decay;
- cooling sequences;
- population-synthesis aging;
- differential stellar ages.

These are stronger than merely converting redshift to lookback time. Their inferred duration depends on physical process models, but the models may be calibrated independently of a chosen cosmological age.

Default allocation: treat as a serious process-depth constraint, not as disposable ΛCDM bookkeeping.

### E3 Cosmological-model duration

Elapsed duration obtained by integrating a cosmological model, for example:

    t(z) = integral dz / [(1+z)H(z)].

These durations can be highly constrained by multiple observations, but the elapsed time remains conditional on the adopted dynamical model and its parameters.

Default allocation: distinguish the observed relation from the inferred absolute duration.

### E4 State/structure evidence

Present structures or inventories whose existence does not alone determine a unique antecedent duration.

Examples may include:

- a mature stellar macrostate;
- a specified abundance inventory;
- large-scale structural configuration;
- initial perturbation spectrum;
- boundary curvature/stress-energy.

Default allocation: initialization remains available if coherent with coupled process evidence and not used to fabricate arbitrary testimony.

## Anti-compression rule

DFM may not shorten a process merely because its conventional duration is inconvenient.

A duration may be reduced only if a developed deployment model changes the relevant physical rate or boundary allocation while preserving the independent observables generated by that process.

Thus:

    model-mediated duration != arbitrary duration,

and

    process evidence != conventional chronology by definition.

## Anti-initialization rule

Initialization may not be used independently for every difficult datum.

If observations form a tightly coupled causal family, the allocation must preserve that family unless a common initialization principle explains the entire structure without arbitrary pseudo-history.

This retains the Real-History Allocation Principle from WP-029C.

## Clock-strength hierarchy

For each claimed age/process duration classify:

**C0:** pure cosmological-model integration.

**C1:** model-mediated physical inference with weak independent calibration.

**C2:** process model calibrated by local/independent observations.

**C3:** directly observed differential/process evolution.

**C4:** directly coupled event chronology with multiple messengers/consequences.

The higher the class, the stronger the burden on any compressed deployment model.

This classification is provisional and must be assigned evidence stream by evidence stream.

## First important correction to the maximal-lapse alternative

The full 13.8-Gyr target cannot simply be dismissed as a ΛCDM artifact.

Stellar population and cosmic-chronometer methods provide process-based differential aging constraints that are not obtained merely by imposing the standard cosmological age-redshift curve.

Conversely, those methods still depend on stellar-evolution/SPS assumptions and therefore do not by themselves establish that every component of the conventional 13.8-Gyr cosmic timeline must execute unchanged.

The minimum-depth programme must therefore confront stellar clocks explicitly.

## Severe-test questions

For each evidence stream:

1. What is actually observed?
2. What causal ordering is directly required?
3. What physical process model maps the observation to duration?
4. How independently is that process model calibrated?
5. What part of the duration comes from cosmological integration?
6. Could initialization coherently supply the starting macrostate?
7. Could extraordinary deployment change the rate without breaking other observables?
8. What coupled observations constrain that change?
9. What is the minimum duration/process depth presently justified?
10. What would falsify the proposed allocation?

## Programme stop rule

If independent process clocks collectively require near-conventional cosmic proper-time depth under any physically coherent deployment model, then the minimum-depth programme converges back toward the full-thermal-history target and the large-lapse burden must be faced directly.

If substantial portions of conventional elapsed history are instead shown to be runtime-model reconstructions rather than independently required process duration, then DFM may allocate those portions to initialized actuality without denying the genuine causal histories that observations require.

Either outcome is acceptable.

## Tier-1 clock ledger: first research pass

### T1-1 Main-sequence turnoff / stellar isochrones

**Observed basis:** cluster color-magnitude distributions, main-sequence turnoff morphology/luminosity, stellar spectra and composition.

**Inference:** stellar structure/evolution models map mass, composition, luminosity, and turnoff state to an elapsed evolutionary age.

**Clock class:** C2 provisionally.

**Why not C0:** the age is not obtained merely by integrating a cosmological expansion history.

**Why not C4:** an old cluster is not watched evolve for billions of years; age is inferred from a physical evolutionary model.

**DFM burden:** a compressed deployment model must either:

1. execute the required stellar evolution under altered but coherent process conditions; or
2. initialize stellar states in a way that also explains the population-level turnoff morphology and its agreement with other clocks.

Initializing individual stars at arbitrary apparent ages is disallowed by the anti-initialization rule.

### T1-2 White-dwarf cooling

**Observed basis:** white-dwarf luminosities, temperatures, luminosity-function termination, cluster membership, atmospheric/interior properties.

**Physical clock:** after nuclear burning ceases, white dwarfs cool by losing residual thermal energy. Cooling models map state to elapsed cooling time.

**Clock class:** C2, with some population-level differential constraints approaching C3 in strength.

**Independent leverage:** white-dwarf cooling and main-sequence turnoff use substantially different physics and are explicitly used as independent stellar-population clocks.

**Known model burdens:** core composition, envelope structure, crystallization/phase separation, initial-final mass relation, atmospheric evolution, and other cooling microphysics.

These are not arbitrary uncertainties. In some clusters, adding identified microphysics such as phase separation reconciles an apparent discrepancy between white-dwarf and turnoff ages.

**DFM burden:** explaining a cool white dwarf by initialization alone is insufficient when a whole cluster simultaneously exhibits a turnoff age, white-dwarf cooling sequence, progenitor/remnant relation, and coherent population history.

### T1-3 Cross-clock convergence

The strongest datum is not either clock separately but their convergence in common stellar populations.

Represent:

    A_MS = F_MS(observables | stellar-evolution model)

    A_WD = F_WD(observables | cooling/progenitor model).

When

    A_MS ~= A_WD

across clusters despite different dominant microphysics, DFM faces a stronger burden than either age estimator alone.

This is a **consilience constraint**, but not an automatic proof that the conventional elapsed duration is actual history. The models can share assumptions or boundary conditions, and both are still retrodictive.

The proper severe-test question is:

> What common physical quantity or genuine history causes two substantially independent clocks to converge?

A deployment model that changes process rates must explain why both clocks transform coherently.

### T1-4 White-dwarf clock severity

Modern reviews characterize cool white dwarfs as useful cosmic clocks because their post-nuclear evolution is dominated by gravothermal cooling. Typical cooling from very hot to very cool states is modeled on gigayear scales.

This gives DFM a difficult rate problem.

If Day-4 deployment compresses white-dwarf cooling by a factor F while preserving ordinary runtime physics after commissioning, the model must specify what physical parameter changed during deployment:

    thermal transport,
    heat capacity,
    crystallization energetics,
    neutrino losses,
    envelope opacity,
    gravitational/temporal geometry,
    or another registered mechanism.

It cannot simply declare the cooling clock accelerated.

### T1-5 Nuclear chronometers

Long-lived radioactive abundance pairs provide a third, substantially different class of clock. Nucleocosmochronology uses known decay physics plus assumptions about initial production ratios and Galactic nucleosynthetic history.

**Clock class:** C2 provisionally.

**Strength:** decay rates are independently measured local physics.

**Weakness/burden:** inferred ages depend on production ratios, nucleosynthesis history, environmental effects for some nuclides, and chemical-evolution assumptions.

The clock therefore does not directly observe billions of years, but it constrains any deployment model that changes decay history.

Important DFM constraint:

**Accelerated nuclear decay remains rejected.**

WP-030 must not reintroduce accelerated decay merely to compress stellar/cosmic chronometers.

Therefore nuclear-clock accommodation must come from initialization/allocation, ordinary genuine process history, or a broader temporal geometry that preserves locally measured decay physics.

### T1-6 Cosmic chronometers

Passive-galaxy cosmic chronometers use differential stellar aging across redshift to infer H(z).

**Clock class:** C2/C3 depending on the specific observable and calibration.

The method is valuable because it does not simply insert a standard cosmological age-redshift curve. But its ages depend on stellar-population synthesis, metallicity, stellar libraries, star-formation histories, and related systematics.

Published systematic studies find SPS-model choice and metallicity to be significant error sources. This confirms that the method is a serious physical clock while remaining model-mediated.

**DFM burden:** a deployment model cannot dismiss cosmic chronometers as circular ΛCDM ages. It must show how the stellar aging relation itself is preserved or transformed.

## First Tier-1 conclusion

The minimum-process-depth programme cannot simply collapse conventional cosmic history to a very short executed interval while leaving local process physics unchanged.

Independent stellar and nuclear clocks would then disagree with their observed states unless one of three things is true:

A. substantial stellar/nuclear history genuinely executed;

B. coherent initialization supplies population states while preserving cross-clock convergence without arbitrary pseudo-history;

C. a common deployment mechanism changes the mapping from local process state to Earth-referenced elapsed time while preserving local physics and the agreement among independent clocks.

Option C returns us toward asynchronous temporal geometry, but WP-029 showed that a 5 x 10^12 universal lapse is physically expensive.

Therefore the key next question is not "Can all stellar history be initialized?" It is:

> **How much of the cross-clock convergence can a coherent initialized stellar population explain without independently fabricating the outputs of multiple physical clocks?**

That is the sharpest current discriminator between DFM initialization and genuine long process depth.
