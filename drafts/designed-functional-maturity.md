## Abstract

Designed Functional Maturity (DFM) is a Christian methodological-designist research programme that treats Genesis 1 as an actual historical description of God's ordered, supernatural, phased deployment of creation. Rather than beginning with a generic mature-creation hypothesis, DFM models successive creation phases as initialization acts that instantiate, configure, populate, and commission domains and systems into ordinary operation. The programme distinguishes deployment causation from the physical laws governing already-deployed systems: the rules that govern a running system need not be the process by which it was deployed. DFM therefore investigates whether a present state that admits a long natural retrodiction must have traversed that reconstructed history. It distinguishes historical age from model-conditioned retrodictive age and holds that retrodictive precision does not by itself establish chronometric certainty without provenance warrant. The programme further expects coherent pre-seeding to produce substantial cross-domain consilience; consilience establishes coherence, while provenance establishes history. Genesis 1's Day 1 light and Day 4 luminary sequence is treated as terrestrial-day continuity: God establishes and constrains the terrestrial light-dark, evening-morning cycle before commissioning the luminaries as its ordinary created governors. DFM retains ordinary observed physical law as the default for post-initialization operation and subjects initialization auxiliaries to severe empirical testing.

**Keywords:** Designed Functional Maturity; methodological designism; retrodiction; initial conditions; radiometric dating; radiogenic heat; philosophy of science; creation; historical inference

## 1. Introduction

Physical sciences routinely infer unobserved past states from present observations. These inferences can be exceptionally powerful. They combine measured present conditions, experimentally established laws, boundary conditions, auxiliary assumptions, and models of system history to reconstruct antecedent states.

DFM asks a narrower epistemic question than a generic rejection of historical science: **when does a successful reconstruction establish the actual historical trajectory rather than one physically admissible trajectory that reaches the observed state?**

The distinction matters whenever the initial state is itself underdetermined by present observation. If a system begins from a deliberately specified mature state rather than from the antecedent state obtained by indefinitely extending its later dynamics backward, then the reconstructed duration can differ from actual elapsed duration even though the governing prospective physics is unchanged.

DFM develops this possibility within a Christian creation framework. The Genesis creation account is treated as supplying real historical boundary conditions, including the deployment of systems capable of immediate commissioned function. Nature remains genuinely evidential. The programme does not use Scripture as permission to ignore physical constraints, nor does it allow natural reconstruction to silently replace the historical boundary supplied by Scripture.

The resulting research task is demanding. DFM must identify which state variables are required for immediate function, which are forced by physical coupling, which are genuine post-initialization event traces, and which observations discriminate coherent initialization from traversed natural history.

The governing maxim is:

> **Physical maturity is a state property. Historical age is a trajectory property. Inferring the latter from the former requires a warranted history model.**

## 2. Programme commitments

### 2.1 Revealed phased deployment

DFM operates within the Biblical World Model and treats Genesis 1 as an actual historical description of God's ordered supernatural deployment of creation. The days are not merely timestamps attached to an otherwise natural assembly process. They describe a real sequence in which domains and systems are instantiated, configured, populated, and commissioned.

The systems-engineering language of rollout, deployment, and run book is explanatory analogy, not a replacement for the biblical text. It highlights a central distinction: operational laws need not be deployment mechanisms.

Schematically:

```text
S_0 --A1--> S_1 --A2--> ... --A6--> S_6
```

and more generally:

```text
S_i = G_i(Phi_i, L, B_i, S_{i-1})
```

where each phase receives an already-established creation and adds the functionality commissioned in that phase.

### 2.2 Terrestrial-day continuity

DFM treats the Genesis day as a terrestrial day. On Day 1 God supplies light, separates light from darkness, and establishes the evening-morning cadence. DFM therefore holds that the initial light is purposefully constrained to establish the terrestrial light-dark cycle before the Sun is appointed.

On Day 4 the luminaries are commissioned to govern and mark the already-established temporal cycle. The terrestrial day therefore precedes its ordinary solar governor. The Day 1-3 and Day 4-6 days are continuous in character and ordinary duration even though the source/governor of illumination changes.

This is canonically coherent with the biblical presentation of divine illumination as not ontologically dependent upon the Sun, including the consummation imagery of Revelation 21:23 and 22:5.

### 2.3 Biblical supernaturalism and methodological designism

Biblical Christianity is ontologically supernaturalist: divine action is a real causal category. DFM therefore does not naturalize the revealed creation transitions merely because ordinary created systems later operate lawfully.

For ordinary operation, however, DFM is methodologically designist. It seeks explanations in the stable causal capacities, laws, structures, boundary conditions, and regularities designed into creation.

```text
supernatural initialization != supernatural ordinary operation
```

### 2.4 Functional maturity

A created system is functionally mature when it possesses the state required to perform its commissioned role at deployment. Functional maturity does not mean maximal development, static perfection, or immunity from subsequent change. It means operational sufficiency at the initialization boundary.

The familiar theological exemplars are straightforward. Adam's adult function does not entail a traversed childhood. The wine at Cana could possess the physical properties of mature wine without having traversed fermentation. The examples establish the conceptual category. They do not by themselves establish any particular physical initialization claim.

### 2.5 Ordinary prospective physics

DFM does not require a second set of physical laws after creation. Its default is:

```text
L_post = L_observed
```

For radioactive decay, DFM adopts the experimentally measured decay behavior as its prospective default (Jaffey et al. 1971; Schoene et al. 2006):

```text
lambda_DFM = lambda_observed
```

Accelerated nuclear decay is therefore not part of the DFM hard core and is not invoked merely to compress a conventional radiometric chronology.

## 3. Initialization formalism

At the Genesis scale DFM uses the phased architecture:

```text
S_i = G_i(Phi_i, L, B_i, S_{i-1})
```

For a local initialized subsystem, the simpler boundary remains:

```text
S_0 = G(Phi, L, B)
```

where:

- `S_0` is the initialized state;
- `Phi` is the set of commissioned functional requirements;
- `L` is the governing law structure;
- `B` is the set of physically coherent boundary conditions.

This notation does not yet specify a complete physical generator. It expresses a constraint architecture. An admissible `S_0` cannot be whatever state happens to preserve a preferred chronology. It must satisfy independently motivated functional and physical constraints.

### 3.1 Functional initialization and state-consistent information

Observed features can provisionally be divided into three classes:

1. **Functional initialization:** state required for immediate operation.
2. **State-consistent initialization information:** state forced or strongly constrained by coherent initialization even if not itself the direct functional output.
3. **Historical traces:** state generated by contingent events after initialization.

The third category is essential. DFM does not claim that all evidence conventionally interpreted historically was created in place.

### 3.2 Functional versus deceptive maturity

The strongest objection to mature creation is deceptive history. If initialization contains arbitrary traces of events that never occurred, the model risks becoming empirically unconstrained.

DFM answers by requiring independent state constraints. A feature is a strong initialization candidate when coherent function, physical coupling, boundary conditions, or the commissioned role requires or strongly constrains it. A feature is presumptively historical when it resembles a contingent event record unnecessary for initialized function and not forced by state coherence.

This criterion is imperfect but testable. It moves the issue from the rhetoric of “appearance of age” to the physical question of what an admissible initialized state actually requires.

### 3.3 Coherent pre-seeding and the virtual-world analogy

An accessible analogy is a pre-seeded virtual world. A competent designer can initialize terrain, hydrology, atmosphere, organisms, astronomical states, and the internal state variables required for coherent operation. Once released, the world follows its ordinary operational rules. Backward extrapolation of those rules can estimate how much runtime would be required to generate aspects of the state naturally, but it does not by itself establish the deployment history.

The analogy is limited. DFM does not assert that physical reality is a simulation. It illustrates state depth, coherent initialization, phased deployment, and provenance.

A pre-seeded world should exhibit consilience. If independently interrogated subsystems were mutually contradictory, that would count against coherent initialization. Thus DFM expects integrated initialized states to agree across domains, while still bearing the burden of explaining the specific structure of that agreement.

## 4. Retrodiction and the Reconstruction Argument

Let:

```text
O_t = observed state at time t
M   = model structure
A   = auxiliary assumptions
B   = boundary conditions
R   = reconstructed trajectory
H   = actual historical trajectory
```

A reconstruction can be represented as:

```text
R = R(O_t | M, A, B)
```

DFM accepts that `R` can be well supported and mathematically precise. It denies that successful reconstruction creates the logical identity:

```text
R = H
```

without additional historical-provenance warrant.

### 4.1 Historical and retrodictive age

DFM therefore distinguishes:

```text
T_H = actual elapsed historical duration
T_R = model-conditioned retrodictive duration
```

The claim:

```text
T_R = T_H
```

is an inference. Retrodictive extrapolation therefore does not necessarily yield chronometric certainty. Its evidential strength depends on how well the initial state, closure history, auxiliary assumptions, and provenance are independently constrained.

### 4.2 Dynamical formulation

Let `F_L^t` represent evolution under law `L`. A backward reconstruction seeks a prior state such that:

```text
F_L^t(S_prior) = O_now
```

DFM permits an initialization boundary that need not be generated by backward extension of the post-initialization dynamics:

```text
S_0 not-in Range(F_L^{t<0})
```

This is not a claim that `S_0` violates `L`. The claim is that the boundary is specified rather than dynamically generated by an earlier state inside the created history.

Consequently, a present state may admit a long retrodictive trajectory without that trajectory being the actual provenance of the initialized state.

### 4.3 Consilience, retrodiction, and provenance

DFM does not treat consilience as surprising under coherent initialization. A deliberately integrated initialized state should exhibit cross-domain consistency. But consilience identifies coherence more directly than provenance.

The programme therefore uses two canonical formulations:

> **Consilience establishes coherence; provenance establishes history.**

> **Measurement establishes state. Retrodiction reconstructs trajectory. Provenance warrants history.**

Where a historical model derives a specific consilient mathematical structure, DFM must still derive or predict its alternative structure. Coherence is not a license for arbitrary pre-seeding.

### 4.4 Underdetermination, not equivalence

The Reconstruction Argument does not imply that all imaginable histories are equally plausible. Independent constraints can make one historical model substantially better supported than another. DFM must therefore engage the full evidential architecture used to establish provenance rather than stopping at the observation that assumptions exist.

## 5. Methodological rules

### 5.1 No-smuggling rule

Measurements and historical inferences must remain distinct.

Isotope ratios, heat flow, spectra, mineral structure, sequences, chemical concentrations, and retention characteristics are observations. Ages and antecedent trajectories reconstructed from them are inferences.

DFM is subject to the same rule. It may not classify a feature as initialization information merely because the conventional historical interpretation is inconvenient.

### 5.2 No-free-lunch rule

```text
T_R != T_H
```

does not license:

```text
S_0 = any state needed to preserve DFM
```

Initialization parameters must be independently constrained.

### 5.3 Anti-circularity rule

DFM cannot insert a conventional target age into isotope initial conditions and then claim recovery of that age as a prediction.

The required direction is:

```text
independent functional + physical constraints
    -> admissible initialized state
    -> predicted present structure
    -> comparison with observation
```

### 5.4 Symmetric auxiliary-hypothesis standard

All research programmes use auxiliaries. DFM therefore does not prohibit model revision either for itself or its competitors. Revisions are evaluated by independent motivation, explanatory reach, predictive novelty, parameter economy, and performance under severe tests.

## 6. Radiogenic systems as a test case

Radiometric dating is a particularly valuable DFM test because nuclear decay physics is experimentally mature, multiple isotope systems can be compared, mineral chemistry constrains initial and subsequent states, and geochronology has developed sophisticated methods for detecting disturbance (Jaffey et al. 1971; Wetherill 1956; Cherniak and Watson 2001; Schoene et al. 2006).

DFM should therefore treat radiometric evidence as a severe test rather than dismiss it as “assumption based.”

### 6.1 Inference architecture

The radiogenic inference can be decomposed as:

```text
O_iso + M_decay + B_initial + G_history -> T_R
```

where:

- `O_iso` is the measured isotope state;
- `M_decay` contains measured nuclear decay behavior;
- `B_initial` specifies initial-state constraints;
- `G_history` describes crystallization, closure, diffusion, alteration, inheritance, mixing, metamorphism, and other geochemical history.

The DFM question concerns the provenance represented by `B_initial` and `G_history`, not the reality of `O_iso` or ordinary decay.

## 7. Radiogenic heat and coherent initialization

Long-lived U, Th, and K isotopes contribute materially to Earth's heat production, a relationship constrained both geophysically and through geoneutrino measurements (KamLAND Collaboration 2011; Huang et al. 2022). An initialized terrestrial system therefore requires a radionuclide inventory compatible with its thermal and geochemical architecture.

For the radiogenic subsystem:

```text
S_0^rad = G(Phi_thermal, L_nuclear, B_geo)
```

A simplified radiogenic heat expression is:

```text
H_R(t) = Sum_i M_i h_i exp(-lambda_i t)
```

where `M_i` is isotope inventory, `h_i` is specific heat production, and `lambda_i` is the observed decay constant.

The heat budget gives DFM an independent reason for a structured long-lived radionuclide inventory. It also constrains the admissible initialization space. A functioning Earth cannot simply receive arbitrary parent abundances.

### 7.1 Coherent-initialization hypothesis

DFM proposes:

```text
S_0^rad -> C_rad
```

where `C_rad` represents structured radiogenic coherence produced by common physical, chemical, mineralogical, and functional constraints.

The key point is that a coherently initialized world should be orderly. Cross-system structure is therefore not intrinsically surprising under DFM.

### 7.2 Orderly marker versus chronometer

Suppose several isotope systems yield:

```text
T_R1 ~= T_R2 ~= T_R3
```

Concordance is evidentially important. DFM cannot answer it by independently tuning every isotope system.

DFM instead investigates whether concordance can follow from a common constrained initialization. If so, concordance can function as an **orderly marker** of the initialized state.

The stronger historical claim requires:

```text
C_rad + provenance warrant -> T_H
```

DFM denies only:

```text
C_rad -> T_H
```

as a logical necessity.

This distinction does not make concordance weak evidence. It specifies what additional work is required to establish it as a chronometer of pre-initialization elapsed history.

## 8. The limits of the heat-budget argument

The radiogenic heat budget is not a universal solvent for geochronology.

It provides direct functional motivation for long-lived U, Th, and K inventory and indirect constraints on their distribution. It does not automatically derive:

- exact mineral-scale parent/daughter ratios;
- U-Pb concordia structure;
- isochron slopes and intercepts;
- extinct-radionuclide daughter excesses;
- closure and reset histories;
- radiation-damage patterns;
- terrestrial-lunar-meteoritic cross-system correlations;
- isotope systems whose parents have no significant heat-budget role.

These are not details to be waved away. They are the places where DFM can be tested.

## 9. Multi-system concordance as a severe test

U-Pb is especially important because two uranium decay chains operate in the same mineral system with distinct decay constants. Zircon generally incorporates U while excluding most Pb during growth, crystalline zircon can retain radiogenic Pb strongly, and concordia/discordia structure supplies internal tests of disturbance (Wetherill 1956; Cherniak and Watson 2001; Valley et al. 2014).

A successful DFM account must therefore do more than say “the initial state was mature.” It must attempt to derive or constrain the observed dual-system structure from a common admissible `S_0` without feeding the conventional target age into the model.

Non-heat-centric systems increase the pressure. Classic meteoritic Rb-Sr work, for example, produced a whole-meteorite isochron broadly concordant with Pb-based Solar System ages (Patterson 1956; Pinson et al. 1965). Rb-Sr, Sm-Nd, Lu-Hf, Re-Os, and extinct radionuclide systems cannot be explained simply by appealing to terrestrial heat requirements. Their order must arise from broader chemical, mineralogical, nucleosynthetic, or initialization constraints if the coherent-initialization hypothesis is correct.

This yields a clear discriminator:

> If independently constrained DFM initialization cannot generate the observed precision and cross-system structure without target-age fitting, the current radiogenic auxiliary loses support.

## 10. Genuine post-initialization chronometers

DFM does not imply that isotope systems never function as clocks.

Events occurring after initialization can produce genuine historical records. If a volcanic event, crystallization event, thermal reset, or other process has an independently established historical age and an isotope system begins or resets under sufficiently constrained conditions, agreement between `T_R` and `T_H` is genuine chronometric evidence.

DFM should preserve such results.

Known-age and independently constrained event calibration is therefore important. It tests both the power and limits of isotope systems under conditions where provenance is available from additional evidence. Cross-method studies such as the Lava Creek Tuff show that Ar-Ar and U-Pb can converge on the same eruption interval while stratigraphic constraints provide additional context (Matthews et al. 2015).

## 11. Cosmological extension

The same framework applies in principle to cosmology.

DFM permits astronomical systems to be initialized in physically mature states capable of their commissioned functions. A present stellar or galactic state may therefore have a retrodictive developmental age greater than its historical age if the initialization boundary is not located on the backward natural-development trajectory.

This does not by itself solve starlight, redshift, supernova, nucleosynthetic, or entropy questions. Each requires its own physical treatment.

### 11.1 Early mature structures

Observations of unexpectedly mature high-redshift structures are relevant only when a specific DFM expectation was established independently of the observation. Merely noting that conventional models were surprised does not constitute a DFM prediction.

A stronger DFM programme should preregister expectations about the degree and kinds of functional maturity expected near its deployment boundary.

### 11.2 Starlight and propagation

“Starlight in transit” is a candidate DFM hypothesis, not a completed explanation. A viable treatment must specify the initialized radiation field, source-state relationship, propagation under ordinary law after initialization, and how the model avoids encoding arbitrary fictional event histories.

### 11.3 Supernova products

Supernovae are relevant both observationally and compositionally. Some products associated with stellar nucleosynthesis contribute to the material inventory required for rocky planets and life. DFM must distinguish the requirement for a functional elemental inventory from the claim that every conventionally reconstructed nucleosynthetic event occurred inside created history.

Observed transient events and remnants occurring after initialization remain genuine history.

## 12. Relationship to low entropy and fine tuning

DFM naturally treats boundary conditions as specified rather than self-generated. Low entropy and life-permitting parameter structure are therefore conceptually compatible with the programme.

Compatibility is not sufficient for corroboration. A DFM model gains explanatory force only when the proposed boundary conditions jointly constrain otherwise independent observables or generate successful predictions.

The programme should therefore resist using “design” as a terminus of explanation. The research question is what physical state a designed functional system requires and what follows from that state.

## 13. Research-programme structure

DFM is developed in a Lakatosian form.

### 13.1 Hard core

The programme protects the following commitments:

1. intentional creation is causally real;
2. biblical creation history supplies historical boundary conditions;
3. functional maturity at initialization is admissible;
4. initialization must be physically coherent;
5. ordinary observed physical law governs subsequent operation by default;
6. retrodictive accessibility does not logically establish historical traversal;
7. empirical observations constrain the protective belt.

### 13.2 Positive heuristic

The programme seeks to:

- define commissioned function;
- identify the minimum coherent initial state;
- derive state variables forced by physical coupling;
- propagate the state forward under ordinary law;
- compare predictions with observation;
- reduce initialization degrees of freedom;
- identify residual anomalies;
- construct severe discriminators.

### 13.3 Negative heuristic

The programme rejects:

- arbitrary initial-state tuning;
- accelerated processes introduced only to compress chronology;
- denial of measurements;
- “created maturity” as a universal escape clause;
- conventional ages smuggled into initialization parameters;
- endless auxiliary repair without increased predictive content.

## 14. Predictions

Current DFM predictions are programme-level and require quantitative sharpening.

### P1. Early functional maturity

Systems may exhibit functional maturity earlier than gradual assembly models predict.

### P2. Global state coherence

Initialized maturity should exhibit physically coupled order rather than arbitrary disconnected “age markers.”

### P3. Ordered retrodictive markers

Measurements coupled to a common initialized architecture may yield mutually ordered retrodictive ages even when those ages do not equal elapsed pre-initialization history.

### P4. Ordinary post-initialization physics

Post-initialization evolution should follow observed physical law except where independent evidence establishes otherwise.

### P5. Disturbance-linked discordance

Radiometric discordance should correlate with independently detectable mineralogical, chemical, thermal, diffusion, inheritance, alteration, or catastrophic-history variables rather than being assigned ad hoc.

### P6. Decreasing initialization freedom

As physical coupling is modeled more completely, the admissible DFM initialization family should become narrower. If explanatory success requires increasing independent free parameters, the programme is moving in the wrong direction.

## 15. Severe tests and potential defeaters

DFM should count the following against a proposed auxiliary:

1. **Age-input circularity:** the target conventional age must be supplied to recover the observed isotope structure.
2. **Uncoupled concordance:** systems lacking a defensible common initialization constraint repeatedly converge more precisely than the admissible DFM state family can generate.
3. **Event-specific provenance:** detailed contingent traces are shown to arise from independently established events and cannot be explained by functional state constraints.
4. **Prospective-law conflict:** an auxiliary requires violations of well-established physics without independent evidence.
5. **Parameter proliferation:** each new observation requires a new independent initialization parameter.
6. **Unique competing prediction:** a competing programme predicts a novel observation in advance while DFM can only accommodate it after the fact.

These criteria do not falsify intentional creation as a theological proposition. They can falsify or seriously degrade specific DFM physical auxiliaries and, cumulatively, can render the research programme empirically degenerative.

## 16. Objections and responses

### Objection 1: DFM is merely “appearance of age.”

**Response:** It would be if initial conditions were unconstrained and inserted solely to mimic arbitrary nonexistent history. DFM instead requires functionally or physically motivated state constraints and explicitly treats contingent event-like traces as a major explanatory burden. Whether the distinction succeeds is an empirical research question.

### Objection 2: Initial conditions can explain anything.

**Response:** Unconstrained initial conditions can. DFM's no-free-lunch and anti-circularity rules are designed to prevent that move. A useful initialization model must reduce degrees of freedom and generate cross-domain consequences.

### Objection 3: Radiometric concordance proves deep time.

**Response:** Concordance is strong evidence for a common structured history or state. DFM agrees that arbitrary independent initialization is inadequate. The narrower question is whether concordance uniquely establishes traversed pre-initialization duration or can arise from a common constrained initialized state. DFM bears the quantitative burden of demonstrating the latter.

### Objection 4: The heat budget cannot explain isochrons or U-Pb concordia.

**Response:** Correct. DFM does not claim that it does. The heat budget independently constrains part of the radionuclide architecture. Mineral-scale and non-heat-centric isotope relationships are severe tests requiring additional derivation from common physical and geochemical constraints.

### Objection 5: This makes historical science impossible.

**Response:** No. Historical inference becomes strong when initial conditions, system closure, event provenance, and independent lines of evidence jointly constrain the trajectory. DFM challenges the logical necessity of backward extrapolation across an intentional initialization boundary, not the legitimacy of retrodiction.

### Objection 6: The model is unfalsifiable because God could create any state.

**Response:** A theological doctrine of divine omnipotence is broader than a scientific research programme. DFM voluntarily restricts its protective belt to independently constrained physical initialization models. Failure of those models under severe tests counts against DFM as an empirical programme even though it does not disprove divine creation.

### Objection 7: Why would creation contain orderly markers that look old?

**Response:** Because functional systems can require physically coupled mature states. Under DFM, order is expected. The relevant question is whether a given marker is forced or independently motivated by coherent initialization or instead represents an unnecessary contingent event trace. DFM must establish that distinction case by case.

## 17. Outstanding explanatory burdens

DFM currently carries substantial unresolved burdens:

- quantitative derivation of U-Pb dual-system concordance;
- extension from heat-producing radionuclides to Rb-Sr, Sm-Nd, Lu-Hf, Re-Os and other systems;
- extinct radionuclide signatures;
- meteorite, lunar, and terrestrial cross-system relationships;
- mineral closure and reset histories;
- detailed criteria separating state-consistent information from contingent event traces;
- a physically explicit Day 4 cosmological deployment model;
- starlight propagation and source-state consistency;
- supernova remnants and nucleosynthetic inventories;
- low-entropy initialization;
- integration of later catastrophic history without ad hoc disturbance assignments.

These are research targets, not solved problems.

## 18. Research agenda

The immediate technical gate is U-Pb.

The programme should:

1. define an admissible U/Th inventory from thermal and geochemical constraints without using radiometric target ages;
2. model U partition into zircon and relevant Pb exclusion/retention behavior;
3. propagate both U decay chains under ordinary measured decay;
4. determine what initial daughter-state family is independently admissible;
5. test whether the observed concordia structure can arise without target-age fitting;
6. quantify residual mismatch;
7. compare the DFM model against conventional provenance models using the same observations and explicit auxiliary accounting.

Subsequent work should apply the same discipline to non-heat-centric systems and extinct radionuclides.

## 19. Conclusion

Designed Functional Maturity proposes a distinction that is conceptually simple but empirically demanding: a mature state and a traversed history are not the same kind of thing.

A present state can encode information from which a long antecedent trajectory is reconstructed. That reconstruction can be mathematically rigorous and empirically well constrained. Yet identifying it with actual history still depends on provenance, especially when the origin boundary itself is under dispute.

DFM introduces intentional, physically coherent initialization as an alternative boundary hypothesis. It retains ordinary prospective physics, rejects arbitrary initial-state freedom, and accepts the burden of explaining why independently measured systems exhibit the order they do.

Radiogenic heat provides a concrete example. Earth's long-lived radionuclide inventory participates in its functional thermal architecture, giving DFM an independent reason to expect a structured radiogenic state. That does not automatically explain geochronology. It creates a constrained starting point from which DFM must attempt to derive the observed concordance structure.

The programme therefore succeeds only if its mature-state hypothesis becomes progressively more constrained and predictive. If coherent initialization can explain multiple independent observations from a common physical state without importing the conventional historical answer, DFM gains empirical content. If it requires increasing ad hoc freedom, it degenerates.

The appropriate question is consequently not whether nature contains order that can be read as history. It plainly does. The question is whether the order uniquely entails the full antecedent history conventionally reconstructed from it.

DFM makes that question explicit and places the burden of answering it on model comparison, provenance, physical constraint, and severe empirical testing.
